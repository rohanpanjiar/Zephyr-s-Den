# The Marriage Green Card — How It Works & What "Expedite" Really Means

## The path: Adjustment of Status via concurrent filing

If Rohan is **physically in the U.S., in valid status, and last entered legally (with
inspection)**, and Meg is a **U.S. citizen**, you qualify for the fastest available route:
**concurrent filing** — submitting the petition and the green card application *together*
so USCIS works them at the same time, and Rohan never has to leave the country.

You file, in one package:

- **Form I-130** — Petition for Alien Relative (Meg petitions for Rohan).
- **Form I-130A** — Supplemental Information about the spouse beneficiary (Rohan).
- **Form I-485** — Application to Register Permanent Residence (Rohan's green card app).
- **Form I-864** — Affidavit of Support (Meg promises to financially support Rohan).
- **Form I-765** — Work permit (EAD) application *(optional but recommended — free to add)*.
- **Form I-131** — Advance Parole travel document *(optional but recommended)*.
- **Form I-693** — Medical exam, sealed by a civil surgeon (can be included or sent later).

Because Meg (assumed U.S. citizen) makes Rohan an **"immediate relative,"** there's **no
visa-number wait / no priority date backlog** — that's what makes this the quick lane.

> If Meg is a **green card holder** rather than a citizen, the spouse category (F2A) can
> have a wait and concurrent filing may not be available until a visa is current. And if
> Rohan is **outside the U.S.** or **entered without inspection**, the route becomes
> **consular processing** instead. Confirm both facts in `08-open-questions.md` — they
> change the plan.

## Why money can't "expedite" the green card itself

- **No premium processing exists** for family-based I-130 or I-485. You cannot pay USCIS
  to go faster the way employment petitions can.
- USCIS will only **expedite** in narrow situations (e.g., documented emergency, severe
  financial loss, urgent humanitarian reasons) — not "we'd like it sooner."
- So total time to the actual green card is roughly **12–20 months** regardless of spend.

## What your money *does* buy

1. **A clean, fast-to-file package.** An immigration attorney or a guided service
   (Boundless/SimpleCitizen) prepares everything correctly in days and handles any
   Request for Evidence (RFE) and the interview. Mistakes are the #1 cause of months of
   delay — paying removes that risk.
2. **A work permit (EAD) in ~3–5 months.** Filing I-765 with the I-485 lets Rohan work
   while waiting (important if his current status/work authorization is time-limited).
3. **A travel document (Advance Parole).** Filing I-131 lets Rohan travel abroad while the
   case is pending without abandoning it. USCIS issues a **combo EAD + Advance Parole
   card**.

## Rough timeline once filed

| Milestone | Typical timing |
|---|---|
| Receipt notices (I-797C) | 2–4 weeks after filing |
| Biometrics (fingerprints) | 3–6 weeks |
| EAD + Advance Parole combo card | 3–5 months |
| Interview + decision (LA field office) | 8–16 months |

If you've been married **less than 2 years** when the green card is approved, the first
card is **conditional** (valid 2 years). You jointly file **Form I-751** to remove
conditions in the 90 days before it expires.

## Travel rules — your specific situation (H-1B + approved I-140)

Because Rohan is on **H-1B** (a dual-intent visa), he **can continue to travel
internationally even with a pending I-485**. He re-enters on his H-1B visa stamp and
valid I-797 approval notice — not on Advance Parole. This is a major advantage over
most other visa categories.

**What to keep in mind:**
- Ensure the H-1B I-797 approval covers the period you'll be traveling. If it expires,
  the employer needs to file an extension or you need a valid H-1B visa stamp.
- Don't let the H-1B *status* expire while the I-485 is pending. If employment ends,
  talk to the attorney immediately — you have a 60-day grace period.
- File **Form I-131** (Advance Parole) in the package anyway as a backup — if anything
  changes with H-1B status mid-process, you'll want the AP card as an alternative.

## The approved I-140 — a hidden asset

Rohan's **approved I-140** (employment-based petition) is worth keeping alive even after
the marriage green card is in process. Two reasons:

1. **Priority date protection.** If something ever delays the marriage-based case and you
   needed to fall back on the EB path, the I-140 priority date is preserved.
2. **H-1B extensions beyond 6 years.** An approved I-140 (and a priority date beyond a
   year) lets Rohan extend H-1B in 3-year increments while AOS is pending. This is
   usually automatic with the employer's attorney — just flag it to them.

Tell the immigration attorney about the I-140 at the first meeting so they can advise on
whether/how to keep it active alongside the marriage case.
